UX Case Study

Redesigning the Pacific Northwest X-Ray Website for Clarity and Speed

The current site presents a radiology equipment catalog as a flat list of links, with contact details buried at the bottom. This study documents a heuristic evaluation of the homepage, the problems it revealed, the wireframe and design decisions that address them, and a planned usability test to validate the result.

RoleUX/UI design (student project)
PlatformDesktop web (1440 px)
MethodHeuristic evaluation, wireframing, design

1. Problem statement and goals

The homepage at www2.pnwx.com is the main entry point for a radiology supply catalog, yet it gives visitors no clear place to start. Fourteen product links sit in one undifferentiated block, the search box has no visible button, and the phone number and address appear only in the footer.

Problem statement: Visitors looking for a specific radiology product or a sales contact cannot quickly find the right category or phone number, because the homepage has no visual hierarchy and no clear primary action.

Goals

  • Make product categories easy to scan from the homepage.
  • Make the phone number and quote request reachable from every screen.
  • Present the brand as current and credible for a business buyer.

2. Research methods

This study used a heuristic evaluation of the live homepage, measured against Nielsen's usability heuristics. The findings are my own observations of the page as it was reviewed, not results from user testing.

What this study does not include yet: No user interviews or usability sessions have been run. The competitive analysis is limited to the current site. The planned test in Section 7 describes how user testing will confirm or revise these findings.

3. Pain point analysis

  1. No starting point. The homepage does not guide a visitor toward the most common task.
  2. Flat product list. Fourteen unlabeled links are hard to scan and compare.
  3. Contact details buried. The phone number appears only at the bottom of the page.
  4. Unclear search action. The search field lacks a visible button.
  5. Dated appearance. The design and copyright year may reduce confidence for a business buyer.

4. Target user

The site describes its catalog as radiology equipment, supplies, and parts, so the likely audience is radiology department staff or purchasing coordinators who order from a known catalog. This is an assumed profile based on the site's content, not a researched persona.

5. Wireframe iterations

Iteration 1: Structure

Low-fidelity grayscale wireframes mapped five sections: navigation, hero with a call to action, feature cards, social proof, and footer. Flows connected the landing page to sign up, onboarding, and features.

Iteration 2: Hierarchy and grouping

The product list was split into five categories so visitors can choose a group before scanning product names. A single search field became the hero's main action.

Iteration 3: Contact

The phone number moved into the header and hero, and a dedicated contact section was added with a short checklist of what to have ready before calling.

6. Final design

The final desktop layout uses a blue primary color, the Inter typeface, and an 8 px spacing scale.

Accessibility

  • Body text #4B5563 on white (about 7.5:1) and headings #111827 on white (about 17.9:1), both above WCAG AA.
  • Visible focus states on links, buttons, and inputs.
  • Skip link, labeled search input, and semantic landmarks.
